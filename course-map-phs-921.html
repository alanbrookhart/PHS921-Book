Causal Inference in Epidemiology Using R

F Course map: PHS 921

This book accompanies PHS 921, and the two are organized on their own terms: the course moves in the order that works for a semester of lectures, the book in the order that works for a reader building one idea on the last. This table lines the two up, session by session, so that a student can find the chapter behind any lecture and a reader can see which lecture a chapter was written for. The lab meets separately and works through the code, so the R material appears here only where a session is built around it.

Session Topic Where in the book
1 Introduction Chapter 1
2–3 Intro to R workshop Chapter 2, Appendix A
4 RCTs and experiments Chapter 4
5 Counterfactuals Chapter 3
6 Study design and immortal time bias Chapter 6
7 Real-world data sources Chapter 2 (the two cohorts), Appendix B
8 Generalized linear models Appendix D; used throughout Chapters 7–9
9–11 Propensity scores and IPTW Chapters 8–9
12 G-computation Chapter 7
13–14 Variable selection and DAGs (+ workshop) Chapter 5; Chapter 8 (choosing the propensity-score model)
15 Censored data and Kaplan–Meier Chapter 10
17 Cox regression, IPCW, competing risks Chapter 11; Chapter 10 (death as a competing risk); Appendix E (Cox regression)
18 IPCW workshop Chapter 11
19–20 Artificial censoring; clone-censor-weight Chapter 12
22 Natural experiments and instrumental variables Chapter 13
23 Negative control outcomes Chapter 14 (negative control outcomes)
24 External control arms Chapter 4 (target trials, and the external control arm paragraph that closes that section)
25–26 Protocol presentations Chapter 4’s protocol table with Chapter 14’s reporting checklist

Two places in the table run backwards relative to the page order. The book introduces causal diagrams (Chapter 5) before study design (Chapter 6), and g-computation (Chapter 7) before propensity scores (Chapters 8–9), because each earlier chapter supplies vocabulary the later one uses. The course takes both pairs in the other order. Where the two disagree, follow the map rather than the page numbers.

Two sessions have no counterpart here. Session 16 is the midterm, and session 21 takes up missing data, which this book does not treat: the two cohorts are complete on the variables we use, and the assumptions and imputation machinery that incomplete data require are a subject of their own rather than a section of a causal-inference chapter. Students should take that session from the lecture notes and the reading assigned with them.
